1. Before you begin
  • Sign in with your administrator account. Every screen in this manual requires you to be signed in.
  • The left-hand menu holds Campaigns, Registration Forms, Post-event Surveys, Users, User Manual (this page — it opens in its own tab, so you can keep it beside the screen you are working on) and Logout.
  • Permissions. Superusers see everything. Everyone else only sees campaigns whose Campaign Team they belong to, and can only build forms and read registrations for those campaigns. If a page returns a permission error, ask a superuser to add you to the campaign team.
Two steps, always in this order. A campaign comes first, then the registration form is attached to it. Creating the form first and attaching it later works, but you lose the event details and email wording that are driven by the campaign until the link is made.

3. Part A — Create a campaign
Step A1Open the Campaigns list

Click Campaigns in the left menu. Each row is one campaign, with its dates, an Active or Inactive badge, and a set of action icons on the right. Use Search and entries per page to narrow a long list.

Step A2Click + Add New

The Add New Campaign dialog opens on top of the list. Nothing is saved until you press Save, so you can back out at any point with Close.

Step A3Fill in the campaign details
Field What to enter
Campaign TitleRequired. Appears in the campaign list, on the dashboard and in registrant emails. If one event has more than one form, name them distinctly — e.g. … - REGISTRATION and … - SPONSORSHIP.
Campaign Start DateClick the field to open the calendar, choose the day, then set the hour and minutes on the bottom row of the picker.
Campaign End DateSame picker. This date is reused as the event date in the confirmation and reminder emails, so set it to the actual event day.
‘Reply To’ EmailWhere replies from registrants go. More than one address is allowed — separate them with commas.
Campaign TeamThe users allowed to manage this campaign and its forms. Click the box and pick from the list; repeat to add several people.
Event Theme ColourAccent colour used on the public form and in registrant emails. Click Choose colour (or the swatch) and pick a colour in your browser’s colour picker. The value must be a 6-digit hex code; anything else falls back to the system default.
Enable or Disable QR Scanning PageLegacy QR submission flow. Leave off unless someone has specifically asked for it.
Active or Inactive the CampaignOn = the campaign is live and shows the green Active badge. Turn it off to retire a campaign without deleting it.
Enable Or Disable Check In InfoOn = registrants are prompted for check-in information. Leave on for events you will check people in at.
Step A4Check the dialog, then Save

Compare your entries against the example below, then press Save. The dialog closes and the new campaign appears in the list.

Step A5Know the row icons

Once the campaign exists, its row carries the icons below. Which ones appear depends on whether a registration form is attached yet.

Icon Action
ChartCampaign dashboard — headline numbers for the campaign.
EyeView registered participants — the registrations list for the attached form.
PencilEdit — reopens the same dialog you used to create the campaign.
GlobeOpen registration form — the live public page, in a new tab.

Step A6The campaign dashboard

The chart icon opens a live summary: total registrations, how many are approved, how many checked in, the no-show count, the registration funnel, registrations over time, top organisations and approval status.

4. Part B — Create a registration form
Step B1Open Registration Forms

Click Registration Forms in the left menu — or click the plus-box icon on a campaign row, which opens this page with that campaign already selected for you.

Step B2Click + New Registration Form and fill it in

The Add New Registration Form dialog opens. Nothing is saved until you press Save.

Field What to enter
Form TitleRequired. Shown as the heading of the public page and in the forms list.
Public URL (optional)The readable part of the public address. Lowercase letters, numbers and hyphens only. Leave it blank and one is generated from the title. It must be unique — if another form already uses it, saving fails with a message telling you so.
Survey Start Date / Survey End DateThe window in which the public form accepts entries. Outside it, visitors see the “registration closed” wording.
Survey DescriptionRich text shown to registrants under the title — venue, closing date, who to contact. The small toolbar handles bold, headings, lists and links.
Select CampaignThe campaign this form belongs to. Pick it here: the event details and email wording in Form Builder depend on it. The list only offers campaigns you are allowed to manage.
Active or Inactive the SurveyLeave on so the form can be opened.
Where is the real link? Don’t hand-build the public URL from this dialog. After saving, open Form Builder — the complete, correct link is displayed at the top under Shareable link, with a Copy button.
Step B3Save — five fields are created for you

When you save a new registration form, the platform seeds the core registrant fields so the form works end to end immediately:

Field Required by default
NameYes
Participant TypeNo — a fixed picklist (Delegate, Speaker, Sponsor, Organizer …)
EmailYes
PhoneNo
OrganizationNo

These are identity fields. They feed the registrations list, the QR confirmation and the check-in screen, so they cannot be deleted — you can only make them optional or reword them.

Step B4Row icons on the forms list

5. Part C — Set up the form in Form Builder

Form Builder has four blocks, top to bottom: the shareable link, the banner, the event details and email content, and the form fields.

Shareable link and banner

Copy puts the public link on your clipboard, Preview opens it without saving anything, Open live opens the real page. The banner sits at the top of the public form: PNG, JPG or WebP, up to 5 MB. Use Replace banner to swap it and Remove to clear it.

Event details

These lines appear in the confirmation and reminder emails, and — if you switch the toggle on — at the top of the public form as well.

DateRead-only. Taken from the campaign’s end date. To change it, edit the campaign.
TimeFree text, e.g. 10.00 am – 1.00 pm.
VenueFree text, multiple lines allowed.
Dress codeFree text.
Additional info rowsExtra lines such as parking, agenda or lucky draw. Each row takes an emoji, a bold label, the text, and an optional link — when a link is given the text becomes clickable. + Add row adds more.
Show event details on this registration formPer-form toggle. On = the block above is also shown at the top of the public page.

Press Save event details when done — this block has its own save button. Event details apply to the whole campaign, so every form on that campaign shares them.

Email content

Four tabs, one per message: Thank-you (sent on submission), QR confirmation, Attendance reminder and Registration closed. For each you can write a subject line and the body.

  • The small buttons — Name, Reg no, Date, Event — drop in a placeholder that is filled per recipient. There is one set for the subject and one for the body.
  • Leave the subject blank to use the system default.
  • Two switches per message: show the banner image at the top, and include the event details block.
  • Sign-off is the closing block — several lines allowed, with bold and colour.
  • Press Save email content, and use Preview to see the rendered email.
Form fields

The question list. Each row shows its number, its type, a Required tag, a List col tag when the answer is shown as a column in the registrations list, and the question text.

  • Drag the handle on the left to reorder; numbering updates itself.
  • Pencil edits a question, bin deletes it.
  • Identity fields (padlock) have no bin — mark them optional instead of removing them.
Add a question

Click + Add Question at the top right of the Form Fields block.

Field What to enter
Question textRequired. Basic HTML is accepted if you need a link or bold inside the label.
Help textOptional one-liner shown under the question.
TypeShort text · Long text (paragraph) · Dropdown · Single choice (radio) · Multiple choice (checkbox).
RequiredOn = the registrant cannot submit without answering.
Show in listOn = the answer becomes a column in the registrations list. Use it for the handful of answers your team sorts and filters on, not for everything.
Column header for the registrations listShort label for that column, up to 60 characters. Blank = the question text, truncated. Only used when Show in list is on.
ChoicesShown for Dropdown, Single choice and Multiple choice. One row per option; + Add choice adds more, the drag handle reorders, the red button removes. Add follow-up text attaches a free-text box to a specific option — useful for “Other, please specify”.
Include an “Other” free-text inputAdds a catch-all “Other” option with its own text box.
Max selectionsMultiple choice only. Caps how many boxes a registrant may tick; blank = no limit.

Press Save Question. The question appears at the bottom of the list — drag it into position. Cancel discards it.

6. Clone a form for a repeat event

When you run the same event again, don’t rebuild the form. Clone last year’s and point the copy at this year’s campaign — every question comes across with its type, choices, required flags and Show in list settings. Registrations do not.

Step 1Click the copy icon on the form’s row

Open Registration Forms, find the form you want to reuse, and click the copy icon — second from the right, just before the red bin.

Step 2Name the copy and choose its campaign
Field What to enter
New Form TitleArrives pre-filled as Copy of …. Replace it with the real name for this year’s event — the copy’s public URL is generated from this title, so a clean title gives a clean link.
Assign to CampaignThe campaign the copy belongs to — normally the new campaign you created for this year. Only campaigns you are allowed to manage are listed.

Press Clone. The list reloads with the new form on it, ready to edit. Cancel leaves everything untouched.

Step 3Fix the three things a copy can’t know
  1. The dates. Survey start and end are copied from the original, so they are still last year’s window. Click the pencil on the new row and set the new one.
  2. The public URL. It is generated from the new title. Open Form Builder and check the link under Shareable link before you share it.
  3. Event details and email wording. These belong to the campaign, not the form, so the copy shows whatever the new campaign holds — usually empty. Fill them in from Form Builder (Part C).
What a clone does and does not carry over
Copied Not copied
Every question — text, help text, type, choices, follow-up boxes, “Other”, max selections, Required, Show in list and its column header · the identity field setup · the form description · start and end dates · active status · the banner image, as its own separate file, so replacing it on one form never touches the other. Registrations and their answers · QR and check-in state · the campaign’s event details and email wording, which live on the campaign rather than the form.
Also useful for testing. To try a change without touching a form that is already collecting registrations, clone it, experiment on the copy, then delete the copy.
7. Test and go live
  1. In Form Builder, click Preview to check the page without writing any data.
  2. Then Open live and submit one real test entry yourself, so you can confirm the thank-you page and the confirmation email.
  3. Check the entry landed in the registrations list, then delete it before you publish the link.
  4. Share the link from Copy — not a URL you typed by hand.
Where registrations arrive

Campaigns → eye icon (or Registration Forms → consolidated report). Each row is one registrant, with the registration number, approval status, check-in and QR state. Header counters summarise Total, Pending, Approved, Rejected, QR sent, Reminder and Checked in. From the toolbar you can register a walk-in, edit an entry, change status or participant type, and send QR codes or reminders.

8. Pre-launch checklist
  • Campaign end date is the real event date — the emails read it from there.
  • ‘Reply To’ email is a mailbox someone actually watches.
  • Everyone who needs access is on the Campaign Team.
  • The form is attached to the right campaign, and its start/end window is correct.
  • Banner uploaded; event details saved; the four emails read the way you want.
  • Required flags are right, and the answers your team works from have Show in list on.
  • One test registration submitted, checked and removed.
  • Campaign is Active and the form is Active.
9. Troubleshooting
Symptom Likely cause
“This URL is already used by another form.”The Public URL you typed is taken. Choose another, or leave it blank to auto-generate.
Permission error opening Form BuilderYou are not a superuser and not on that campaign’s team. Ask to be added.
The campaign does not appear in Select CampaignSame reason — the dropdown only lists campaigns you may manage.
Wrong date in the emailsThe date comes from the campaign’s end date. Edit the campaign, not the form.
Event details missing from the public pageShow event details on this registration form is off, or Save event details was not pressed.
Registrants see “registration closed”Today is outside the form’s start/end window, or the form or campaign is inactive.
Theme colour ignoredThe value was not a valid 6-digit hex code, so the default was used.
An identity field has no delete buttonBy design. Make it optional instead.
“Permission denied” when cloningYou need rights over both ends: the form you are copying and the campaign you are copying it into.
A cloned form shows last year’s datesExpected — dates are copied with the questions. Edit the new form and set its own window.
